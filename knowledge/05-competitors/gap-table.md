## Where LARIS is differentiated
1. Bidirectional loop: supplier quotes feed a cost book with source receipt and date per line, and outgoing quotes check margin against it. No checked competitor shows this. Caveat: SQL Account, AutoCount and Million are unverified and may have purchase-quotation modules.
2. WhatsApp-first intake of supplier PDFs, photos and voice notes. Accounting tools are forms; WhatsApp tools lack quote logic.
3. Auditable pricing: AI only extracts, rules price, every cost line traceable.

## Where LARIS is NOT differentiated
- Outgoing quotes, PDF sharing and accept/decline tracking are commodity (Xero, Zoho, Wave).
- Bukku already sells OCR extraction; document capture is not unique, only quote-specific capture is.
- WhatsApp Business app is free, entrenched and has catalog, quick replies and labels; switching cost is behavioural.
- MYR fit, MyInvois e-invoice compliance and the accountant relationship sit with Bukku, AutoCount, SQL Account. LARIS has none yet.
- BM/Manglish: no competitor evidence either way; an unproven advantage until tested.
- Auto follow-up exists in generic CRMs; not verified for quotes here.

## Strongest differentiators
1. Supplier quote to cost book to margin-guarded outgoing quote, with receipt/date provenance.
2. WhatsApp voice/photo capture into structured lines.
3. Rules-priced, AI-extract-only design.

## Biggest threats
1. WhatsApp Business app plus a WhatsApp CRM (SleekFlow, ChatDaddy) adding quote features.
2. Malaysian accounting incumbents (Bukku, AutoCount, SQL Account) adding AI/WhatsApp capture to existing customer bases. Unverified, check urgently.
3. Platforms like Orbyte (AI OS with Connect integrations) or global tools (Zoho, Xero) bundling quoting cheaply.

Coverage gaps: SQL Account, AutoCount, Million, Jobber, Joist, QuickBooks MY, Zoho MYR, Malaysian WhatsApp order tools need REAL DATA (search tool was rate-limited).
