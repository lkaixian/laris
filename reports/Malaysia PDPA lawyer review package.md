# Malaysia PDPA: what LARIS must do, what is proven, what a lawyer must confirm

**Not legal advice.** This is research by a student team for a Malaysian lawyer to confirm. Law is as of 30 Oct 2026. Labels: **[P]** = the note's author read the official text (Act, Gazette, Commissioner PDF); **[S]** = a law-firm or press summary only. Several official documents are Malay-only and the quotes are the researchers' own translations.

LARIS is covered by the PDPA, and the 2024 amendment made it riskier for a small SaaS, because it now counts as a processor with its own criminal exposure. The Act applies to personal data handled "in respect of commercial transactions", which includes supplying goods or services, so SME quotations are in scope and the Act has no SME exemption ([P] [Act 709 reprint](https://www.pdp.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/UNDANG-UNDANG-MALAYSIA_AKTA_PERLINDUNGAN_DATA_PERIBADI_2010_709_MALAY_AND-ENG_V2022.pdf), ss.2, 4, 45). Since 1 April 2025 a processor must itself follow the Security Principle, and breaching any of the seven principles carries up to **RM1,000,000 and/or 3 years** (up from RM300,000/2 years) ([P] [Act A1727](https://www.pdp.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Akta-A1727-bm.pdf) s.4; [Gazette P.U.(B) 522](https://www.pdp.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/PENETAPAN-TARIKH-PERMULAAN-KUAT-KUASA.pdf)). The figures the repo flagged as unverified are now verified from the Commissioner's own PDFs: the **72-hour** Commissioner deadline, the **7-day** data-subject deadline, the **more-than-1,000** scale test and the **2-year** breach register ([P] [Circular 2/2025](https://www.pdp.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Pekeliling-DBN.pdf), [DBN Guideline](https://www.pdp.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/GP_DBN_BM.pdf)). The DPO thresholds are also verified: more than 20,000 data subjects, more than 10,000 with sensitive data, or regular and systematic monitoring ([P] [DPO Guideline](https://www.pdp.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/GP_DPO_ENG.pdf)). Still open are when the 72-hour clock starts, whether the Commissioner's thresholds bind legally, whether the "continue to message us" consent works, which legal basis covers overseas AI and hosting vendors, and whether LARIS's JSON export counts as access or portability. Those questions are what the lawyer package should be built around. The repo's privacy text has at least seven concrete gaps (section 5), the biggest being that the customer notice is not in both Malay and English.

## The disagreement between the notes is mostly a reading-depth problem

Two conflicts need settling. On breach timing, the breach note read the primary Commissioner's Circular 2/2025 and the Guideline (both PDFs on pdp.gov.my) and quotes them directly. The sensitive/AI note read only the statute plus a DBN user manual (lines 40 to 110, where grep for "72" found nothing) and so called the numbers secondary-only. The DPO note's section 4 also said the DBN Guideline was "NOT read". Rule applied: the note that opened the primary text wins. So 72 hours, 7 days, 1,000 and 2 years are **[P]**. The "secondary-only" label in the other two notes is out of date, not contradicted. The 2-year figure needs one caveat. The sensitive/AI note says the Act has no register requirement, which is correct: it comes from the Guideline (para 14.1) and Circular 4(7), not the statute.

On DPO thresholds, the DPO note read the English Guideline (para 4.2) and the Malay Circular (para 4(1)) directly, so the thresholds are **[P]**. The notice note says it never opened the DPO circular and calls the 20,000 figure **[S]**; that is a gap in that note, not a counter-finding. It also notes the Act itself has no size carve-out, which is true and is exactly why the thresholds' legal force is a lawyer question. The DPO note also found one number, "2,000", appearing in no source, and treats it as wrong. A smaller conflict is official numbering: the DPO Circular is called No. 1/2025 on its cover and by the DPO Guideline but No. 2/2025 on the pdp.gov.my page title; the breach Circular is consistently 2/2025. Cite by title and URL, and ask the lawyer which number to use.

These points remain unresolved after reconciling:
- **Clock start.** Guideline 6.1 says 72 hours "after occurrence"; the worked examples in 6.2 start the clock when the controller is told, confirms the breach, or (with a processor) is told by the processor or has clear evidence, whichever is earlier. Law-firm summaries split (DLA Piper: occurrence; CMS: occurrence or when informed) ([S] [DLA Piper](https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2025/03/malaysia-guidelines-issued-on-data-breach-notification-and-data-protection-officer-appointment/), [CMS](https://cms.law/en/sgp/legal-updates/new-guidelines-on-mandatory-breach-notifications-under-malaysia-s-personal-data-protection-act-2010)).
- **Register start.** The Guideline counts 2 years from notification to the Commissioner; the Circular counts from the breach date ([P], internal inconsistency).
- **Effective-date text.** Circular 2/2025 section 10 did not extract, so 1 June 2025 rests on the Gazette and law firms.
- **Statute copies.** Three notes read A1727 from different copies (pdp.gov.my Malay PDF, a pdp.gov.my English copy, a law-firm-hosted Gazette copy). No note read an official consolidated post-amendment Act 709; the English text of unamended sections is the pre-amendment V2022 reprint.

## What the amended Act asks of a small chat-based SaaS

**Dates.** Gazette P.U.(B) 522 (dated 19 Dec 2024) phased A1727 in: 1 Jan 2025 for admin sections; **1 Apr 2025** for the "data controller" rename, the biometric and personal-data-breach definitions, processors under the Security Principle, the higher s.5(2) penalty and the rewritten cross-border rule (s.129); **1 Jun 2025** for the DPO duty (s.12A), breach notification (s.12B) and data portability (s.43A) ([P] [P.U.(B) 522](https://www.pdp.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/PENETAPAN-TARIKH-PERMULAAN-KUAT-KUASA.pdf)).

| Topic | What the sources say | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Breach: who and what | s.12B binds the **controller**. "Personal data breach" = any breach, loss, misuse or unauthorised access. Statute: tell the Commissioner "as soon as practicable"; tell people "without unnecessary delay" if significant harm is likely. | [P] A1727 s.3, s.6 |
| Breach: numbers | Commissioner within **72 hours**; people within **7 days after** the Commissioner notice; notify Commissioner if significant harm OR more than **1,000** people; people are told on significant harm only (the 1,000 test does not apply to them). Late notice needs written reasons and evidence. | [P] Circular 2/2025 paras 3-5; Guideline 5-9 |
| "Significant harm" | Not defined in the Act. Circular/Guideline treat it as: physical harm, financial loss, credit-record damage, property loss; likely misuse; **sensitive data**; identity-fraud combinations; or more than 1,000 people. | [P] Circular 4(3); Guideline 5.2-5.3 |
| Breach content, register, plan | Notice lists time aware, data types, cause, count, system, consequences, chronology, measures, DPO contact; can be phased within 30 days. Keep a register of **all** breaches, including non-notifiable, for at least 2 years; have a response plan and run training. Report via the pdp.gov.my form or dbnpdp@pdp.gov.my; the Commissioner's acknowledgement is needed for the notice to count. | [P] Guideline 7, 11, 14; portal [pdp.gov.my/lapor-dbn](https://www.pdp.gov.my/lapor-dbn/) |
| Encryption | No express safe harbour. It works only through the harm test (Guideline 8.3 example: strongly protected data means people need not be told, but the Commissioner still must be). A lost decryption key is itself a breach. | [P] Guideline 4.2(viii), 5.4, 8.3 |
| Processor in a breach | s.12B does not bind processors directly. The controller must bind the processor **by contract** to notify promptly and assist. | [P] Guideline 12.1-12.2; Circular 7(2) |
| Penalties | Failing to notify the Commissioner: up to **RM250,000 / 2 years** (s.12B(3)). Failing to notify people (s.12B(2)): no stated offence (inference). Principle breaches: up to **RM1m / 3 years**, now including processors. Officers of a company are deemed liable under s.133 unless they prove no knowledge and due diligence (s.133 is unchanged). | [P] A1727 s.4, s.6; Act 709 s.133 |
| DPO: duty | s.12A: controllers and processors must each appoint DPOs. The Guideline limits mandatory appointment to more than **20,000** data subjects, or sensitive (incl. financial) data of more than **10,000**, or **regular and systematic monitoring**. Below those, an entity "may keep a record on the reasons for not appointing". No size-based exemption exists as such. | [P] A1727 s.6; [DPO Guideline](https://www.pdp.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/GP_DPO_ENG.pdf) 4.2-4.5; [Circular](https://www.pdp.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Pekeliling-DPO.pdf) 4(1) |
| DPO: mechanics | Controller registers the DPO at daftar.pdp.gov.my within **21 days**, updates within **14 days** of a change. DPO may be part-time, outsourced or shared, needs no formal qualification, must be Malaysia-resident (180 days) or easily contactable, and fluent in Malay and English. Publish a dedicated DPO email via website, notices or policies. No stated offence for failing to appoint (not verified either way). | [P] DPO Guideline 5-7, 15-16 |
| Processor duties | s.5(1A): processor must follow the Security Principle (s.9, which now binds "a data controller and a data processor"). Final consolidated s.9(2) wording was not obtained. Expected controls come from the Personal Data Protection Standard 2015 (security, retention, integrity); its security detail is in images and was not read, and revisions are only at consultation ([S] [Baker McKenzie](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/alerts/2025/08/malaysia-public-consultation-on-the-personal-data-protection-regulations)). | [P] A1727 ss.4-5 |
| Notice (s.7) | Written notice with eight items: data and processing, purposes, source, access/correction rights and contact, third-party classes, choices to limit processing, obligatory or voluntary, consequences. Given at first collection or request. Must be in **both the national language and English**. A1727 did not change s.7. The JPDP guide says the notice is not a consent tool and consent "must be obtained separately, recorded and maintained". | [P] Act 709 s.7; [JPDP Notice Guide](https://www.pdp.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Panduan-Penyediaan-Notis-PDP-2022-compressed.pdf) |
| Consent | s.6(1): consent needed for ordinary data, with exceptions including steps requested by the data subject toward a contract (s.6(2)(b)), which plausibly fits a customer asking for a quote. Sensitive data needs **explicit consent** or another s.40 condition. The regulation on how to record consent (Regulations 2013) was not retrievable. | [P] Act 709 ss.6, 40; regulation [S]/unread |
| Rights | Access and correction: **21 days**, extendable by up to 14 more days with written reasons; refusal grounds in s.32. Withdrawal of consent (s.38): processing must stop; offence up to RM100,000/1 year. Direct-marketing opt-out (s.43). **Portability** (s.43A, in force 1 Jun 2025): written electronic request that the controller transmit data directly to another controller; the completion period is "as may be prescribed" and no prescription was found. Access fees: RM2 to RM30 depending on type ([S], PDF not opened). There is **no standalone erasure right**. | [P] Act 709 ss.30-43; A1727 s.9 |
| Retention | s.10: do not keep data longer than necessary; destroy or permanently delete it when no longer needed. No fixed period. The 2015 Standard adds: keep a disposal record, dispose of commercial-transaction forms within 14 days unless legally needed, and a 24-month schedule for inactive data. | [P] Act 709 s.10; [Standard 2015](https://www.pdp.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/LatestStandard.pdf) |
| Cross-border | Since 1 Apr 2025 the Minister's whitelist is gone. A controller may transfer abroad if the destination has a substantially similar law or equivalent protection (s.129(2)), or under an exception: consent, contract necessity, legal proceedings, avoiding adverse action, "all reasonable precautions and due diligence" (via binding corporate rules, contract clauses or certification), vital interests. JPDP's guideline (29 Apr 2025) makes a Transfer Impact Assessment an optional but expected record, valid up to 3 years; contract necessity is read narrowly; transfers must be disclosed in the notice and logged in a register (recipient, country, data types, purpose, evidence). | [P] A1727 s.12; [CBPDT guideline](https://www.pdp.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/BUKU-GARIS-PANDUAN-PEMINDAHAN-DATA-PERIBADI-RENTAS-SEMPADAN-CBPDT-BM.pdf) (Malay) |
| Sensitive / biometric | A1727 adds "biometric data" (personal data from technical processing of physical, physiological or behavioural characteristics) to sensitive data. Whether a transcribed voice note or an ordinary photo counts is **settled by no source**. Face recognition would clearly count. Chat text may incidentally contain health or offence data, which are already sensitive. | [P] A1727 s.3; question open |
| AI | The Act has **no** automated-decision or profiling right. JPDP's draft AI framework (Consultation Paper 1/2026, 5-23 Oct 2026) would require the notice to disclose AI use, new notice before new AI purposes, a necessity check for sensitive data, controllers deciding if an AI vendor is a processor, and s.129 treatment with a TIA for overseas AI. It is a **draft with a blank issue date**. A 2025 ADM/profiling paper and 2026 DPIA, privacy-by-design and ADM guidelines exist; the DPIA one was read, the ADM ones were not. MOSTI AI guidelines are voluntary and an AI Governance Bill is only at consultation. | [P] draft: [CP 1/2026](https://www.pdp.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Kertas_Konsultasi_Awam_Bil_1_2026_Kerangka_AI_Perlindungan_Data_Peribadi-1.pdf); ADM and AI-bill items [S] ([Allen & Gledhill](https://www.allenandgledhill.com/mm/publication/articles/30468/s-personal-data-protection-department-launches-public-consultations-on-guidelines-for-data-protection-impact-assessments-data-protection-by-design-and-automated-decision-making-and-profiling), [Baker McKenzie](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/10/malaysia-consultation-on-proposed-ai-and-personal-data-protection-framework)) |

**Role split.** LARIS is probably a **processor** for each SME customer (the controller) and a **controller** for its own owner accounts. The lawyer must confirm this per data set. Territory also matters: a Malaysian company is "established in Malaysia" under s.2(2)/(4), and the Act excludes data processed wholly abroad unless it is intended for further processing in Malaysia (s.3(2)) ([P] Act 709). For a quotation tracker the data comes back to Malaysia, so assume the Act applies. For a processor with several SME clients, the DPO notes admit the rules do not say whether the 20,000 count is per tenant or aggregate. The DPO note's practical advice is to name a part-time or outsourced DPO anyway, since it costs little.

**Other instruments to ask about.** The DPIA Guideline (2026) uses the same 20,000/10,000 numbers plus qualitative factors, and its own example is storing customer data with an external cloud provider, so a lightweight DPIA is "best practice" ([P] [DPIA Guideline](https://www.pdp.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/GARIS-PANDUAN-PENILAIAN-IMPAK-PERLINDUNGAN-DATA-DPIA-1.pdf)). A Commissioner Circular 1/2026 on data controller registration exists but was not read, so whether LARIS or its SME customers must register is unknown. A draft amendment to the 2013 Regulations (Consultation 4/2025) may or may not be final.

## The repo audit as a checklist

Paths are under C:/LARIS. Items are in rough order of legal risk. The audit covered the privacy notice, runbook, breach code and UI text. The AI-settings notice in `ai_guide.py`, `src/webhooks` code and other web files were not read.

1. **Notice not in both languages.** `src/api/bot_text.py:9,25` and `docs/privacy-and-breach-runbook.md:82` give English or Malay by bot language; WhatsApp uses English. s.7(3) requires both national language and English. Fix: show both together or give a link to a bilingual notice.
2. **Notice misses s.7(1) items.** `src/api/bot_text.py:15-18` (en) and `:34-38` (ms) cover data and purposes loosely and third parties generically, but omit the right to correct, obligatory or voluntary supply, the choices to limit processing, any overseas processing, and a named contact officer. Add the JPDP-recommended retention period and named recipients too.
3. **"By continuing to message us you accept this."** `src/api/bot_text.py:16-18`. No source backs this as consent; the JPDP guide says consent must be separate, recorded and not blanket. The runbook itself flags it (`:85`, `:89`). Consider a gated first-contact flow with a recorded opt-in (user ID, timestamp, notice version, language, exact text).
4. **Deadline wording.** `docs/privacy-and-breach-runbook.md:26,84` say "the time the law gives (check the vault note)". Replace with 21 days for access and correction, with a 14-day extension path (s.31, s.35). The 3-day pending warning (`:84`) is a product choice. Remove the claim that an erasure deadline exists.
5. **Breach deadlines in code.** `src/api/privacy.py:20-22` says "as we understand the guideline". 72h/7d are now **[P]**, so reword. `:107` computes `detected_at + 72h`; consistent with the examples in Guideline 6.2, not with the literal 6.1 wording. Ask the lawyer, and consider conservative handling (earliest of being told or having clear evidence).
6. **People-notice logic.** `:108` sets `commissioner_notified_at + 7 days` only if `significant_harm`; this is right. Check the 1,000 test is never applied to people notices (`:22,106`; UI `web/src/Privacy.tsx:116`). The owner tick box (`Privacy.tsx:83`) should ask the Guideline's harm factors (financial loss, misuse, sensitive data, identity fraud), not a single tick.
7. **Breach register.** `src/api/privacy.py:73-81`, `web/src/Privacy.tsx:10-14,168`. The 2-year rule is now **[P]** (`runbook:47`). Check the register logs non-notifiable breaches too and add fields the Guideline wants (cause, system affected, chronology, measures). Mention the official portal and the dbnpdp@pdp.gov.my route in the runbook (`:49-52`); the "confirm at pdp.gov.my" hedge (`Privacy.tsx:175`) is fine.
8. **DPO handling.** `web/src/Privacy.tsx:40` says the law "asks a business to publish a contact person"; publication is a Guideline duty only for those who appoint. `:249` calls the contact a "DPO"; keep that word only if a DPO is actually appointed and registered at daftar.pdp.gov.my (21 days). `runbook:9` raises `privacy_dpo_missing` even for entities below the thresholds, stricter than the law; keep it as a choice but document the reason. `bot_text.py:19,39` and `knowledge/04-regulation/pdpa-malaysia.md:14` are consistent with the statute. Update `runbook:87-89` and the knowledge file to show the 1 June 2025 start and thresholds as verified.
9. **Portability versus access.** `runbook:27` and `src/api/privacy.py:185` label a JSON export "Access / portability". It answers an access request; s.43A needs controller-to-controller transmission on a written electronic request. Relabel, and ask the lawyer whether LARIS (as processor) or the SME handles s.43A. `bot_text.py:23,43` (/mydata) is access, so the 21-day clock applies. `runbook:85` includes owner notes in /mydata; check against the s.32 refusal grounds.
10. **Erase versus keep.** `Privacy.tsx:244-245,299-300`, `runbook:59-62` and `bot_text.py:20-22,40-42` keep quotes, lines, dates and prices "as financial records". No legal basis has been checked, and there is no erasure right, so wording like "erased" must be accurate. Ask which Malaysian record-keeping law supports this and for how long. The s.10(2) duty to delete data no longer needed still applies. Whether deletion must flow to sub-processors (`Privacy.tsx:300`, `runbook:62`) is unknown.
11. **Retention periods.** `runbook:17,66,111`: chat text 180 days, supplier replies 180 days, images 90 days, `ai_calls` 90 days. These are design choices without a legal source. Put the periods in the notice and keep a disposal log, per the 2015 Standard.
12. **Cross-border and AI vendors.** `runbook:20,78,113` (ILMU, RunPod, Meta, Telegram, Mailtrap, Replit; "agreements and data regions are not confirmed") and `bot_text.py:15-17` (does not say processing may be abroad). Choose an s.129 basis for each foreign recipient and start a transfer register (CBPDT para 16). A US destination is a weak fit for a "substantially similar law" TIA, so the practical routes are recorded consent after notice naming the recipient class, or contract clauses. `runbook:95,101` and `docs/technical-debt.md:11` admit masking misses addresses and non-contact names; masking is not a legal test.
13. **Notice versioning and AI disclosure.** `runbook:82`: customers are not re-shown a changed notice. The draft AI paper (7.3-7.4) would want AI use disclosed and a new notice before new AI purposes; this is draft only. `docs/technical-debt.md:20` (row 10): the v2 RAG notice was not read by a lawyer and consent is recorded per business, not per customer. Keep the human review step for AI drafts; if prices are ever sent without review, ADM becomes relevant.
14. **Processor contracts.** Every customer contract (and every vendor contract) should carry: security measures, prompt breach notice to the controller, sub-processor controls, deletion or return, help with data subject requests, and audit rights. This is an inference from the Guideline's contract expectation, not a verified template.
15. **Knowledge file.** `knowledge/04-regulation/pdpa-malaysia.md:21` (consultation "5 Oct 2026", not law) and `:24-25` (processor role; voice note not biometric, unconfirmed) match the research. Add the verified 72h/7d/20,000/10,000 figures with their sources.

## Draft lawyer-review package

The goal is that a Malaysian lawyer can answer in an hour or two rather than starting from scratch. Keep it to a few pages plus attachments.

### Cover brief (one page)

Say who LARIS is: a student hackathon team's quotation tracker for SMEs, with Telegram and WhatsApp chat, voice-note transcription and AI drafting, used by SME owners whose customers message the bot. State that the team is not asking for a formal opinion on the whole business, only to confirm the points below. Give the facts that change the answer: LARIS is probably a processor for each SME and a controller for its own owner accounts; expected number of data subjects (far below 20,000 today); categories collected (names, phone numbers, chat text, voice notes, photos, quote history); no deliberate sensitive or biometric processing; vendors and where they sit (ILMU, RunPod, Meta, Telegram, Mailtrap, Replit; hosting regions unconfirmed); the 72-hour runbook; human review before any AI-drafted quote is sent. State plainly that the team has researched the law from official sources and now wants the lawyer to confirm or correct it.

### Numbered questions

1. **Role.** Is LARIS a data processor for each SME, a controller for its own account data, or both? Does that change by data set?
2. **Consent.** Does a bilingual notice plus "continuing to message us" count as consent for ordinary data, or does LARIS need a separate recorded opt-in? Does s.6(2)(b) (steps toward a contract) cover quote requests? What does Regulation 3 of the PDP Regulations 2013 require when consent is recorded?
3. **Sensitive data.** Chat text and voice notes may incidentally include health or offence information. Is explicit consent needed up front, and how should the bot handle it?
4. **Biometric.** Is a voice note that is only transcribed and discarded, or a photo used for OCR or product matching, "biometric data" under the new definition?
5. **Notice.** Is the bilingual (Malay + English) requirement met by two language blocks or a link? Can the notice be delivered inside a chat in a compact form? Which s.7(1) items must appear in the chat itself rather than behind a link?
6. **Breach clock.** Does the 72 hours start on occurrence or on awareness or confirmation? Is the safe rule "earliest of being told or having clear evidence"? Which register start date applies (breach date or notice date)?
7. **Breach role.** As a processor, what must LARIS do beyond telling the controller promptly? Is a hard hour limit in contracts sensible (for example 24 hours)? Does a processor have any direct s.12B exposure?
8. **People notices.** Is it right that failing to notify data subjects has no direct criminal penalty? How should the significant-harm test be applied for small incidents?
9. **DPO.** Are the Commissioner's thresholds (20,000 / 10,000 / regular and systematic monitoring) legally binding given that s.12A is unconditional? For a multi-tenant processor, are data subjects counted per tenant or in total? Does logging user behaviour in a SaaS amount to "regular and systematic monitoring"? Is a DPO still advisable below the thresholds, and what is the penalty for not appointing?
10. **Registration.** Does Circular 1/2026 require LARIS, or its SME customers, to register as data controllers?
11. **Rights.** Who answers access, correction and withdrawal requests, LARIS or the SME? Is a chat command an acceptable "written notice by electronic means"? Is a JSON export enough for an access request? Has the s.43A completion period been prescribed, and does portability apply to a processor?
12. **Retention and erasure.** Which Malaysian laws justify keeping quotes after a "forget" request (and for how long)? Are the 180- and 90-day periods acceptable? Must deletion be passed to sub-processors?
13. **Cross-border.** For each foreign vendor, which s.129 basis is workable (consent, contract clauses, other)? Is a Transfer Impact Assessment needed? Is ILMU domestic? What contract clauses and certifications does the lawyer accept?
14. **AI.** Does the draft AI framework change anything today? Is disclosing AI use in the notice advisable now? Are processors that call AI vendors responsible for treating them as processors or third parties?
15. **Contracts.** Please review a template processor agreement (breach notice, sub-processors, deletion, audit) and the vendor DPAs.
16. **Penalties.** Confirm the exposure: RM250,000/2 years for failing to notify, RM1m/3 years for principle breaches, officer liability under s.133. What would a regulator expect from a student team's prototype?
17. **Currency.** Please check against the official consolidated Act 709 as amended, the English version of the DBN and CBPDT guidelines, and anything issued after 30 Oct 2026, including whether the AI paper and the ADM guideline are final.

## Conclusion

The research moves the team's problem from "we don't know the numbers" to "we know the numbers; are our product choices legally enough?". The statute itself gives almost no figures. The 72-hour, 7-day, 20,000 and 10,000 numbers all live in Commissioner instruments, and their legal weight (binding rule or guidance) is the single most useful question to put to a lawyer, because it decides whether a small SaaS is exempt from the DPO duty and how hard the clock rules bite. The second finding is that processor status is not a shield: LARIS carries RM1m criminal exposure for security failures in its own right, so customer contracts and vendor agreements matter as much as the chat notice.

Of everything the audit found, the cheapest wins are the bilingual notice, correct deadline wording and honest labels, and a separate recorded consent, none of which needs a lawyer to start on. The hardest open points (overseas AI vendors, the "continue to message" consent, and biometrics for voice and photos) have no official answer, so the package should lead with them. Remember that the AI framework and the other 2026 guidelines may still change, so anything the lawyer confirms should carry a date.
