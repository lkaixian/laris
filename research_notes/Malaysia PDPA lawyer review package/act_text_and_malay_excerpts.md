## 2(a). When does the 72-hour clock start?

### Takeaway
The Malay Guideline para 6.1 says the notice must be made within 72 hours "selepas berlakunya" (after the occurrence of) the breach. The para 6.2 examples then run the clock from when the controller is told, realises or confirms the breach after a preliminary investigation. The official English Guideline renders 6.1 as "no later than seventy-two (72) hours from the occurrence". Confidence: high on the wording; medium-low on which reading a regulator would apply.

### Cited Findings
- [P] Guideline 6.1 (Malay): "Pemberitahuan hendaklah dibuat dengan secepat yang dapat dilaksanakan dalam tempoh tujuh puluh dua (72) jam selepas berlakunya pelanggaran data peribadi tersebut." — Translation: "Notification shall be made as soon as practicable within seventy-two (72) hours after the occurrence of that personal data breach." — [GP_DBN_BM.pdf](https://www.pdp.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/GP_DBN_BM.pdf). Confidence: high (verbatim from the PDF text layer).
- [P] Official English Guideline 6.1: "The notification shall be made as soon as practicable and no later than seventy-two (72) hours from the occurrence of the personal data breach." — [GP_DBN_ENG.pdf](https://www.pdp.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/GP_DBN_ENG.pdf). Confidence: high. (Earlier note in this folder said no English version existed; that was wrong, an official English PDF is linked from the English Guideline page.)
- [P] Guideline 6.2 (Malay): "Setelah pengawal data dimaklumkan oleh individu, organisasi media atau mana-mana sumber lain, atau apabila pengawal data sendiri mengesan insiden keselamatan, pengawal data hendaklah menjalankan penyiasatan awal bagi memastikan sama ada pelanggaran data peribadi benar-benar telah berlaku." — Translation: "Once the controller is informed by an individual, a media organisation or any other source, or itself detects a security incident, the controller shall conduct a preliminary investigation to establish whether a personal data breach has actually occurred." Confidence: high.
- [P] 6.2 examples (Malay, key phrases): (i) lost unencrypted USB: "tempoh 72 jam ... bermula sebaik sahaja kehilangan tersebut dimaklumkan kepada pengawal data" (starts as soon as the loss is reported to the controller); (ii) misdirected data: "bermula sebaik sahaja pengawal data secara tidak sengaja menghantar data peribadi tanpa kebenaran dan menyedari kesilapannya" (starts when the controller realises the error); (iii) suspected intrusion: "bermula sebaik sahaja pengawal data semasa memeriksa sistemnya, mengesahkan bahawa sistem tersebut sebenarnya telah terjejas" (starts when the check confirms compromise); (iv) ransomware: starts "apabila pengawal data menyedari bahawa mereka telah kehilangan akses kepada data mereka" or, if told by the attacker, after it "menjalankan penilaian sendiri dan mengesahkan bahawa pelanggaran data peribadi telah berlaku" (own assessment confirms); (v) processor involved: "bermula sebaik sahaja pemproses data memaklumkan pengawal data ... atau apabila pengawal data sendiri memperoleh bukti jelas ... mengikut mana-mana yang terdahulu" (starts when the processor informs the controller or the controller has clear evidence, whichever is earlier). Confidence: high on text, English glosses mine.
- [P] Circular 2/2025 para 4(4) (Malay): "Dalam tempoh tujuh puluh dua (72) jam daripada pelanggaran data peribadi, pengawal data hendaklah mengemukakan kepada Pesuruhjaya maklumat seperti yang berikut" — "Within seventy-two (72) hours from the personal data breach, the controller shall submit to the Commissioner the following information". Para 4(4)(a)(i) asks for "tarikh dan masa pelanggaran data peribadi disedari oleh pengawal data" (date and time the breach was noticed by the controller). Para 4(1): "Pengawal data yang mempunyai sebab untuk mempercayai bahawa pelanggaran data peribadi telah berlaku, hendaklah dengan secepat yang dapat dilaksanakan memberitahu Pesuruhjaya". — [Pekeliling-DBN.pdf](https://www.pdp.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Pekeliling-DBN.pdf). Note: the Circular PDF's own title is "Bilangan 2 Tahun 2025 (Pemberitahuan Pelanggaran Data)", but the English-language pdp.gov.my page labels the same PDF "Circular No. 1/2025 (Data Breach Notification)" and the DPO circular "No. 2/2025". The PDFs themselves say DBN = Bil. 2/2025, DPO = Bil. 1/2025 (the DPO guideline 3.1 and 4.1 cite "Pekeliling Bil. 1/2025"). Use the PDF numbering.
- [S] Law firms: DLA Piper says 72 hours from occurrence; CMS says occurrence or when informed — [DLA Piper](https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2025/03/malaysia-guidelines-issued-on-data-breach-notification-and-data-protection-officer-appointment/); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/sgp/legal-updates/new-guidelines-on-mandatory-breach-notifications-under-malaysia-s-personal-data-protection-act-2010). I did not re-read these this session; carried from the earlier note.

### Inferences
- The Act's trigger is "has reason to believe a personal data breach has occurred". A conservative reading is that the clock starts at the earliest point the controller (or a processor telling it) has reason to believe, and that "preliminary investigation" time is inside the 72 hours, not before it. The Guideline examples favour awareness/confirmation, the 6.1 headline words favour occurrence. A lawyer should settle this.

### Gaps
- No Commissioner statement or enforcement decision resolving the tension. Circular para 10 (effective date) is not text-extractable (page image).

## 2(b). The 7-day data-subject deadline

### Takeaway
Seven days run from the notification to the Commissioner, not from the breach. Confidence: high.

### Cited Findings
- [P] Guideline 9.1 (Malay): "Pemberitahuan kepada subjek data yang terjejas seperti yang dirujuk dalam perenggan 8.1 hendaklah dibuat tanpa kelengahan yang tidak perlu, tidak lewat daripada tujuh (7) hari selepas pemberitahuan pelanggaran data dibuat kepada Pesuruhjaya di bawah perenggan 7.1." — "Notification to affected data subjects as referred to in para 8.1 shall be made without unnecessary delay, not later than seven (7) days after the breach notification is made to the Commissioner under para 7.1." Official English 9.1: "...not later than seven (7) days after the initial data breach notification is made to the Commissioner under paragraph 7.1." Confidence: high.
- [P] Circular 2/2025 para 5(2) (Malay): "Dalam masa tujuh (7) hari selepas pemberitahuan pelanggaran data dibuat oleh pengawal data kepada Pesuruhjaya, pengawal data hendaklah mengemukakan kepada subjek data yang terjejas maklumat-maklumat seperti yang berikut" — "Within seven (7) days after the controller notifies the Commissioner, the controller shall provide to the affected data subjects the following information". Para 5(1): notify "tanpa mengira skala signifikan" (regardless of significant scale). Confidence: high.
- [P] Guideline 8.2 (Malay): the "significant scale" criterion (para 5.3, more than 1,000) "tidak terpakai" (does not apply) in deciding whether data subjects must be notified.

### Inferences
- If a controller never notifies the Commissioner (e.g. wrongly decides no significant harm), the 7-day clock is never formally triggered; the statutory standard "without unnecessary delay" in s.12B(2) still applies.

### Gaps
- None for the text itself.

## 2(c). Breach register retention: 2 years from when?

### Takeaway
Circular and Guideline conflict. Circular 4(7): two years from the date the breach occurred. Guideline 14.1: two years from the date of notification to the Commissioner. Confidence: high that they differ; no source reconciles them.

### Cited Findings
- [P] Circular 2/2025 para 4(7) (Malay): "Pengawal data hendaklah menyenggarakan suatu rekod berhubung pemberitahuan pelanggaran data peribadi dalam bentuk fizikal dan elektronik. Rekod tersebut hendaklah disimpan selama sekurang-kurang dua (2) tahun dari tarikh pelanggaran data peribadi berlaku." — "The controller shall maintain a record of personal data breach notifications in physical and electronic form. The record shall be kept for at least two (2) years from the date the personal data breach occurred."
- [P] Guideline 14.1 (Malay): "Pengawal data hendaklah menyimpan rekod dan menyenggara daftar yang memperincikan pelanggaran data peribadi untuk tempoh sekurang-kurangnya dua (2) tahun dari tarikh pemberitahuan pelanggaran data peribadi kepada Pesuruhjaya termasuk pelanggaran yang tidak memenuhi kriteria pemberitahuan kepada Pesuruhjaya dan/atau subjek data yang terjejas." — "The controller shall keep records and maintain a register detailing personal data breaches for at least two (2) years from the date of notification of the personal data breach to the Commissioner, including breaches that did not meet the criteria for notifying the Commissioner and/or affected data subjects." The official English 14.1 matches ("from the date of the notification to the Commissioner").
- Also in the Guideline, not the Circular: the register covers non-notifiable breaches (14.1) and must record the justification for not notifying (14.1.5, "justifikasi untuk tidak membuat pemberitahuan"). The Circular text speaks only of records "berhubung pemberitahuan" (about notifications).

### Inferences
- For an unnotified breach, "from the date of notification" has no start date, so the Circular's "date of occurrence" is the only workable anchor. Safe practice: keep the longer of the two, i.e. the later of occurrence + 2 years or notification + 2 years.

### Gaps
- None found that resolves the discrepancy (no FAQ entry on retention).

## 2(d). DPO mandatory thresholds and the "may record reasons" clause

### Takeaway
Three alternative triggers: more than 20,000 data subjects; sensitive personal data including financial information for more than 10,000 data subjects; or activities requiring regular and systematic monitoring. Guideline 4.5 says the controller or processor "may" (not "shall") keep a record of reasons for not appointing. Confidence: high.

### Cited Findings
- [P] Circular 1/2025 para 4(1) (Malay): "Bagi maksud seksyen 12A Akta 709, pengawal data dan pemproses data hendaklah melantik seorang atau lebih pegawai perlindungan data dalam mana-mana kes jika pemprosesan melibatkan: (a) data peribadi melebihi 20,000 bilangan subjek data; (b) data peribadi sensitif termasuk maklumat kewangan melebihi 10,000 bilangan Subjek Data; atau (c) aktiviti yang memerlukan pemantauan berkala dan sistematik." — "For the purposes of section 12A, controllers and processors shall appoint one or more DPOs in any case where the processing involves: (a) personal data exceeding 20,000 data subjects; (b) sensitive personal data including financial information exceeding 10,000 data subjects; or (c) activities requiring regular and systematic monitoring." — [Pekeliling-DPO.pdf](https://www.pdp.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Pekeliling-DPO.pdf) (same text as the "circ" copy). Confidence: high.
- [P] DPO Guideline 4.2 (Malay) repeats it: "4.2.1 data peribadi melebihi 20,000 bilangan subjek data; 4.2.2 data peribadi yang sensitif termasuk maklumat kewangan melebihi 10,000 subjek data; atau 4.2.3 melibatkan aktiviti yang memerlukan pemantauan data peribadi secara berkala dan sistematik." Official English 4.2 matches. — [GP_DPO_BM.pdf](https://www.pdp.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/GP_DPO_BM.pdf); [GP_DPO_ENG.pdf](https://www.pdp.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/GP_DPO_ENG.pdf)
- [P] Circular 1/2025 para 3(1)(g)-(h) (Malay) define the terms: "pemantauan berkala" = processing "berlaku secara berterusan atau dijalankan secara berulang ... pada masa yang ditetapkan" (continuous, or repeated at set times); "pemantauan sistematik" = processing "diatur, teratur dan berkaedah" (arranged, orderly, methodical).
- [P] DPO Guideline 4.3 gives examples (behavioural-advertising profiling; retail site algorithms; telecom networks; wearables health data; CCTV and connected devices) and a loyalty programme that "mungkin tidak" (may not) count if used only to manage accounts.
- [P] DPO Guideline 4.5 (Malay): "Pengawal data atau pemproses data boleh menyimpan rekod alasan untuk tidak melantik pegawai perlindungan data sekiranya mendapati syarat-syarat dalam perenggan 4.2 tidak dipenuhi." — "A controller or processor may keep a record of the reasons for not appointing a DPO if it finds that the conditions in paragraph 4.2 are not met." Official English 4.5: "may keep a record on the reasons for not appointing data protection officer if they find the requirements in para 4.2 are not fulfilled." Confidence: high. It is permissive, not mandatory.
- [P] Related: Guideline 4.4 / Circular 4(2): notify the Commissioner of an appointment "sekiranya terdapat apa-apa keperluan yang mendesak" (if there is any urgency); Circular 8(2): register the DPO within 21 days of appointment; 8(3): update within 14 days if a DPO leaves.
- [P] The pdp.gov.my English FAQ repeats the 20,000 / 10,000 / monitoring triggers and says "If the data controller or data processor meets the above criteria, a DPO must be appointed and registered." — [FAQ](https://www.pdp.gov.my/en/faq/) (page modified 2025-07-18).

### Inferences
- The Act s.12A(1)-(2) has no threshold; it says "shall appoint". The thresholds come only from the Circular/Guideline, which raises the question in (e) of whether the thresholds are an authoritative limiting construction or only guidance.

### Gaps
- "Sensitive personal data including financial information" is not defined further; whether "financial information" is a sub-category or an extension is not explained in the text read.

## 2(e). Is the Guideline binding or best practice? Legal status of Circulars and Guidelines (s.48, s.104)

### Takeaway
Neither the Guideline nor the Circular states that it is "binding" or "best practice". They are issued under the Commissioner's s.48(g) function, which expressly mentions "circulars". The Circulars use mandatory "hendaklah" (shall) and the Guideline says it is read with the Circular; whether non-compliance with them is itself actionable is a legal question I could not settle from the texts. Confidence: high on what the texts say; no view on enforceability.

### Cited Findings
- [P] Guideline 2.1 (Malay): "Garis Panduan yang dibangunkan oleh Pesuruhjaya adalah selaras dengan fungsi Pesuruhjaya di bawah subseksyen 48(g) Akta 709." — "Guidelines developed by the Commissioner are in line with the Commissioner's function under subsection 48(g) of Act 709." Same in DPO Guideline 2.1 (English: "issued ... pursuant to the function and responsibilities of the Commissioner under subsection 48(g)").
- [P] Guideline 1.4 (Malay): "Garis Panduan ini hendaklah dibaca bersama dengan Akta 709, Pekeliling ... Bilangan 2 Tahun 2025 ... dan mana-mana instrumen undang-undang yang dikeluarkan di bawah Akta 709. Garis Panduan ini tidak mengatasi mana-mana undang-undang atau peraturan-peraturan perlindungan data khusus lain yang berkuat kuasa." — "This Guideline shall be read together with Act 709, Circular No. 2/2025 ... and any legal instrument issued under Act 709. This Guideline does not override any other specific data protection laws or regulations in force." Para 1.3: examples are "bukanlah bersifat menyeluruh" (not exhaustive, illustrative).
- [P] Text search of the Malay and English DBN Guideline and DPO Guideline found no words equivalent to "binding", "mengikat", "best practice" or "amalan terbaik". The Guideline uses "hendaklah" (shall) throughout, e.g. 6.1, 9.1, 14.1.
- [P] Act 709 s.48(g) (V2022 reprint; A1727 s.10 deletes only s.48(e)): "The Commissioner shall have the following functions: ... (g) to monitor and supervise compliance with the provisions of this Act, including the issuance of circulars, enforcement notices or any other instruments to any person". The Act has no section saying that a guideline or circular has the force of law. Note: s.48(g) lists "circulars" but not "guidelines" by name; "any other instruments" is the hook.
- [P] s.104 (V2022): complaint provision, "Any individual or relevant person may make a complaint in writing to the Commissioner about an act, practice or request-- ... (d) that may be a contravention of the provisions of this Act, including any codes of practice." It does not mention circulars or guidelines. s.105 allows investigation on a complaint; the Circular 8 and Guideline 7.9 cite s.105, and Guideline 7.10 cites s.121 for calling for breach records. s.108 (enforcement notice) is the Act's compulsory tool (not read in full this session).
- [P] A1727 s.14(1): pre-commencement Commissioner "guideline[s] ... shall continue to be valid" as made under the amended Act (saving provision; it does not convert later guidelines into law).
- [P] The Act itself imposes the duty and penalty: s.12B(3) for failing to notify the Commissioner (RM250,000 / 2 years). s.12B(1) delegates "manner and form" to the Commissioner ("in the manner and form as determined by the Commissioner"), and Circular 2/2025 para 4(2) says the manner and form are "ditentukan oleh Pesuruhjaya dalam Garis Panduan" (determined in the Guideline). This is the strongest textual link making the Guideline's form requirements operative. The 72-hour and 7-day numbers are not in the Act.
- [S] No secondary source read this session addresses legal status; the Baker McKenzie May 2026 alert on newer guidelines does not discuss it ([Baker McKenzie](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/05/malaysia-new-personal-data-protection-guidelines)).

### Inferences
- Reasonable lawyer questions: (1) is the 72-hour period a valid "manner and form" determination under s.12B(1), or an extra-statutory requirement; (2) are the DPO thresholds a lawful limit on a s.12A duty that has none on its face. I found no case law or Commissioner statement answering either.

## 2(f). Are a processor's data subjects counted across customers?

### Takeaway
Not found. No text read says whether a processor adds up data subjects across all its controller customers or counts per controller. Confidence: n/a (silent).

### Cited Findings
- [P] The Circular 1/2025 4(1) and Guideline 4.2 trigger applies to "pengawal data dan pemproses data" (controllers and processors) alike when "pemprosesan melibatkan" (the processing involves) more than 20,000 data subjects. The text says "processing involves", with no per-customer qualifier and no aggregation rule.
- [P] DPO Guideline Part D / FAQ: one DPO may serve several organisations; FAQ: "data controller or data processor may appoint a single Data Protection Officer (DPO) to serve multiple organisations, taking into account the functions, structure, and size of those organisations" — [FAQ](https://www.pdp.gov.my/en/faq/). This is about sharing a DPO, not counting.
- [P] FAQ on processors: the controller must ensure the processor guarantees security measures; the processor has security duties (s.9 as amended). No count rule.
- Searched the English and Malay DPO Guidelines for "aggregate", "across", "customers", "total": no counting rule found.

### Inferences
- The literal text ("the processing involves ... exceeding 20,000 data subjects") is silent, so a processor serving many small customers whose combined records exceed 20,000 is, on the plain words, processing more than 20,000 data subjects. That is my reading, not an authority.
