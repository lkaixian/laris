## 1. Which section introduced breach notification, when did it take effect, and by what gazette notice?

### Takeaway
New section 12B of Act 709 (inserted by section 6 of Act A1727) creates the duty. Under Gazette P.U.(B) 522, section 6 of A1727 came into operation on 1 June 2025. The "personal data breach" definition (s.3 of A1727, amending s.4 of Act 709) came earlier, on 1 April 2025.

### Cited Findings
- Act A1727: Royal Assent 9 Oct 2024, published in Gazette 17 Oct 2024. Section 1(2): commences on dates the Minister appoints by Gazette notification, with different dates allowed for different provisions. [PRIMARY] — [Act A1727 PDF](https://www.pdp.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Akta-A1727-bm.pdf)
- Section 6 of A1727 inserts new Division 1A of Part II after s.12: s.12A (data protection officer) and s.12B (data breach notification). [PRIMARY] — same PDF
- Gazette P.U.(B) 522, dated 19 Dec 2024, published 24 Dec 2024 by the Attorney General's Chambers, signed by Gobind Singh Deo, Minister of Digital: (a) 1 Jan 2025 for ss.7, 11, 13, 14 of A1727; (b) 1 Apr 2025 for ss.2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 10, 12; (c) 1 Jun 2025 for ss.6 and 9. [PRIMARY] — [P.U.(B) 522](https://www.pdp.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/PENETAPAN-TARIKH-PERMULAAN-KUAT-KUASA.pdf)
- Mapping: s.6 (new ss.12A DPO and 12B breach notification) and s.9 (new s.43A data portability) both start 1 June 2025. s.3 (new definitions of "personal data breach", "biometric data"; data subject excludes deceased) and s.4/s.5 (processor must comply with Security Principle; raised s.5(2) penalty) start 1 April 2025. s.2 (rename "data user" to "data controller") starts 1 April 2025. s.12 (s.129 cross-border transfer rewrite) starts 1 April 2025. s.13 (e-service) and s.14 (saving) start 1 Jan 2025. [PRIMARY] derived from the two sources above.
- The Commissioner's Circular No. 2/2025 and the Guideline (v1.0, issued 25 Feb 2025) implement s.12B. The Guideline says it is read with Circular 2/2025 and is issued under Commissioner's function in s.48(g). [PRIMARY] — [Guideline PDF (Malay)](https://www.pdp.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/GP_DBN_BM.pdf); [Circular 2/2025 PDF (Malay)](https://www.pdp.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Pekeliling-DBN.pdf)
- Law firms also report 1 June 2025 as the breach-notification start. [SECONDARY] — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/sgp/legal-updates/new-guidelines-on-mandatory-breach-notifications-under-malaysia-s-personal-data-protection-act-2010); [DLA Piper](https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2025/03/malaysia-guidelines-issued-on-data-breach-notification-and-data-protection-officer-appointment/)
- Official breach-reporting portal and form: https://www.pdp.gov.my/lapor-dbn/ ; guideline channel also includes dbnpdp@pdp.gov.my and hard copy (Guideline para 7.1). [PRIMARY]

### Inferences
- The user's recollection "1 Jan / 1 Apr / 1 Jun 2025" is correct and the gazette notice is P.U.(B) 522. Breach notification is the 1 June 2025 tranche.
- The Circular's own section 10 ("Tarikh kuat kuasa") did not extract as text; I could not read its stated effective date. The 1 June 2025 date rests on the Gazette plus secondary sources.

## 2. Threshold: "personal data breach", "significant harm", what counts, what a notification must contain

### Takeaway
"Personal data breach" is broad (any breach, loss, misuse or unauthorised access). Notification to the Commissioner is required where the breach causes or is likely to cause significant harm, or is of significant scale (more than 1,000 data subjects). Notification to data subjects is required only on significant harm; the 1,000 test does not apply to them.

### Cited Findings
- s.4 Act 709 as amended: "personal data breach" means any breach of personal data, loss of personal data, misuse of personal data or unauthorized access of personal data. [PRIMARY] — A1727 s.3(d)
- s.12B(1): where a data controller "has reason to believe that a personal data breach has occurred", it shall, as soon as practicable, notify the Commissioner in the manner and form the Commissioner determines. The statute itself contains no "significant harm" qualifier for the Commissioner and no hour count. [PRIMARY] — A1727 s.6
- s.12B(2): where the breach "causes or likely to cause any significant harm to the data subject", the controller shall notify the data subject, in the form determined by the Commissioner, "without unnecessary delay". [PRIMARY] — A1727 s.6
- The statute does not define "significant harm". The Circular (para 4(3)) and Guideline (para 5.2) deem a breach to cause or be likely to cause significant harm if there is a risk that the affected data: (i) may result in physical harm, financial loss, negative effect on credit record, or damage/loss of property; (ii) may be misused for unlawful purposes; (iii) includes sensitive personal data; (iv) includes personal data plus other personal information that could enable identity fraud; or (v) is of significant scale. [PRIMARY] — [Circular](https://www.pdp.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Pekeliling-DBN.pdf); [Guideline](https://www.pdp.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/GP_DBN_BM.pdf)
- "Significant scale" = more than 1,000 affected data subjects (Circular 3(1)(e); Guideline 5.3). [PRIMARY]
- Guideline para 5.1: not every breach must be notified; the controller notifies the Commissioner only if the breach results or is likely to result in "significant harm" (as defined by the list above, which includes scale). Para 5.5: assess against the 5.2 criteria; if any is met, notify. [PRIMARY]
- Guideline para 8.2: the same meaning of significant harm applies for data subject notice, but the "significant scale" criterion (5.3) does NOT apply when deciding whether to notify data subjects. Circular 5(1) says data subjects must be told where significant harm results "regardless of the significant scale". [PRIMARY]
- Guideline worked examples: lost laptop with customer data - notify if the dataset is likely to cause significant harm or exceeds 1,000; unauthorised access to patient medical records - notify (sensitive data); stolen encrypted laptop with 200 employee emails - no notification; hospital records unavailable after cyberattack - notify; email of customer account statement to wrong recipient - notify (financial information). [PRIMARY] — Guideline para 5.4
- Content of notice to the Commissioner (Circular 4(4); Guideline 7.4): date and time the controller became aware; types of personal data and nature of breach; method of identification and suspected cause; number of affected data subjects; estimated records affected; affected system; likely consequences; chronology of the incident; measures taken or proposed to address the breach and mitigate adverse effects; measures for affected data subjects; DPO or other contact details. Submission via the form on pdp.gov.my, or Appendix B form by email to dbnpdp@pdp.gov.my or hard copy. If not all information is available, give it in phases as soon as practicable and no later than 30 days from the initial notice (Circular 4(6); Guideline 7.5). The Commissioner issues an acknowledgement and the notice is treated as not received without it (Guideline 7.3). [PRIMARY]
- Content of notice to data subjects (Circular 5(2); Guideline 10.2): information on the breach; likely consequences; measures taken or proposed by the controller including mitigation; steps the data subject can take to eliminate or reduce adverse effects; DPO or other contact for more information. Individually and directly, in understandable language (Guideline 10.1); sent separately from newsletters or routine messages (10.5). Alternative public communication (website notice, print media, official social media, push notification) is allowed where direct notice is impracticable or needs disproportionate effort (Circular 5(4); Guideline 10.3-10.4). [PRIMARY]
- Each controller notifies separately where more than one controller is involved (Guideline 7.6). [PRIMARY]
- Controller must keep a breach register for at least 2 years, including breaches that did not meet the notification criteria (Guideline 14.1). Clock differs between the two documents: Guideline says 2 years from the date of notification to the Commissioner; Circular 4(7) says 2 years from the date the breach occurred. [PRIMARY, internal inconsistency]
- Controller must have a breach management and response plan and run periodic training and simulations (Guideline 11). [PRIMARY]
- Notification under Act 709 applies in addition to other laws' notification duties (PDRM, sector regulators BNM/SC/MCMC, NACSA/NCII sector leads under the Cyber Security Act 2024) (Guideline 15-16). [PRIMARY]

### Inferences
- The runbook's rule "notifiable if significant harm OR more than 1,000 people" is correct for the Commissioner. It is wrong if applied to data subjects (see section 3).
- Because "sensitive personal data" is itself a significant-harm trigger (and A1727 s.3(c) adds biometric data to sensitive personal data), most breaches touching health, religious, political, offence-related or biometric data are notifiable regardless of volume.

### Gaps
- No official English text of the Guideline; translations here are mine. I did not locate the Guideline's definition of "negative effect on credit record" or any threshold for "financial loss".

## 3. Deadlines, when the clock starts, exemptions

### Takeaway
The statute says only "as soon as practicable" (Commissioner) and "without unnecessary delay" (data subjects). The 72 hours and 7 days come from the Commissioner's Circular 2/2025 and Guideline, not from the Act. 72 hours to the Commissioner; data subjects within 7 days after the notice to the Commissioner. The runbook's "72 hours and 7 days" is correct as a description of the regulator's rules, with the clock-start caveat below.

### Cited Findings
- Commissioner: "as soon as practicable within 72 hours after the occurrence (berlakunya) of the personal data breach" (Guideline 6.1). Circular 4(4): the information must be submitted "within 72 hours of the personal data breach". [PRIMARY] — [Guideline](https://www.pdp.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/GP_DBN_BM.pdf); [Circular](https://www.pdp.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Pekeliling-DBN.pdf)
- Clock start, as worked in Guideline 6.2 examples: after an alert or detection, the controller does a preliminary investigation to establish whether a breach actually occurred. The 72 hours begins (i) when a lost unencrypted USB drive is reported to the controller; (ii) when the controller sends data in error and realises it; (iii) for suspected intrusion, when the controller's check confirms compromise; (iv) for ransomware, when the controller realises it lost access or, if told by the attacker, completes its own assessment and confirms the breach; (v) where a processor is involved, when the processor tells the controller or when the controller itself has clear evidence, whichever is earlier. [PRIMARY]
- Tension: 6.1 text reads "after occurrence", while the 6.2 examples run from awareness or confirmation. The statute's trigger is "has reason to believe a breach has occurred". Law firms differ: DLA Piper says 72 hours from occurrence; CMS says occurrence or when informed. [SECONDARY] — [DLA Piper](https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2025/03/malaysia-guidelines-issued-on-data-breach-notification-and-data-protection-officer-appointment/); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/sgp/legal-updates/new-guidelines-on-mandatory-breach-notifications-under-malaysia-s-personal-data-protection-act-2010)
- Late notice: if not notified within 72 hours, the controller must give written reasons for the delay with supporting evidence (timeline, internal communications, technical or external factors) (Circular 4(5); Guideline 7.7). Lateness is therefore addressed but not excused. [PRIMARY]
- Data subjects: "without unnecessary delay, and not later than 7 days after the notification to the Commissioner" (Guideline 9.1); Circular 5(2): within 7 days after the controller notifies the Commissioner. The 7-day clock runs from the Commissioner notice, not from the breach. [PRIMARY]
- Data subject notification is excused or replaced in one respect only that I found in primary text: disproportionate effort allows public communication instead (Circular 5(4); Guideline 10.3). [PRIMARY]
- Encryption: there is no express encryption exemption in the Act, Circular or Guideline. It operates only through the harm test. Guideline 8.3 example: criminals hacked a pharmacy supplier's server, but customer data was protected by two layers of security rendering it unintelligible, so data subjects need not be told; the Commissioner still must be told "in the prescribed manner". The 200-employee encrypted laptop example in 5.4 needs no notice at all because no significant harm is likely. A lost decryption key counts as a breach (Guideline 4.2(viii)). [PRIMARY]
- CMS describes data-subject notice as excusable where measures render data unintelligible. [SECONDARY] — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/sgp/legal-updates/new-guidelines-on-mandatory-breach-notifications-under-malaysia-s-personal-data-protection-act-2010)

### Inferences
- Safe operating rule: treat the clock as starting at the earliest of the controller (or its processor) being told or having clear evidence, and file within 72 hours of that, because the 6.1 wording could be read against a controller who delays "confirmation".
- Runbook check (C:/LARIS/docs/privacy-and-breach-runbook.md):
  - Line 49, "72 hours after `detected_at`": consistent with the awareness-based examples in Guideline 6.2 but not with the literal wording of 6.1 ("after occurrence"). Lawyer should confirm.
  - Line 50, "7 days after the Commissioner notice when significant harm is likely": correct.
  - Line 49, "significant harm, or more than 1,000 people": correct for the Commissioner only. It must not trigger data-subject notices (Guideline 8.2).
  - Line 89 lists these as unverified, which can now be updated: 72 hours and 7 days are verified from the official Circular and Guideline; "significant harm vs 1,000" is verified (1,000 is itself one limb of the significant-harm test for Commissioner notice; Commissioner-only); 2-year register is verified but the start date is inconsistent between Circular and Guideline. The statute itself has no hour counts.

### Gaps
- No primary-source statement of a general encryption safe harbour; none exists in the texts read. No definitive statement resolving "occurrence" versus "awareness".

## 4. Duty on data users (controllers) only? What must a processor do?

### Takeaway
The s.12B notification duty falls on the data controller only. The Guideline says it does not apply directly to processors; the controller must bind the processor by contract to notify it promptly and assist. Separately, processors now have a direct Security Principle duty and criminal exposure under the amended s.5.

### Cited Findings
- s.12B uses "data controller" only. [PRIMARY] — A1727 s.6
- Guideline 12.1: mandatory s.12B notification "does not apply to data processors directly". 12.2: controller must impose by contract on the processor obligations to notify the controller promptly of a breach and to provide all reasonable and necessary assistance. Circular 7(2) says the same (by contract or other reasonable means). [PRIMARY]
- Processor-to-controller timing is not set in hours; Guideline 6.2(v) starts the controller's 72 hours when the processor informs the controller, or when the controller has clear evidence, whichever is earlier. [PRIMARY]
- New s.5(1A): a processor processing on behalf of a controller shall comply with the Security Principle in s.9. s.9(1) as amended: "a data controller and a data processor shall" take practical steps to protect personal data. A processor that contravenes s.5(1A) commits an offence under s.5(2). New s.12A(2) requires processors to appoint a DPO. [PRIMARY] — A1727 ss.4, 5, 6 (note: s.9(2) amended text in A1727 s.5 shifts the processor-contract duty to the processor: "a data controller, the data processor shall...")
- DLA Piper reports the same contractual position. [SECONDARY] — [DLA Piper](https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2025/03/malaysia-guidelines-issued-on-data-breach-notification-and-data-protection-officer-appointment/)

### Inferences
- A processor failing to alert its controller is not itself in breach of s.12B(3), but may be in breach of the contract and arguably the Security Principle.
- LARIS role matters: as a vendor processing for SME customers, LARIS is a processor; as owner of its own user/owner accounts it is a controller. The lawyer should confirm the role split per data set.

### Gaps
- I did not extract the amended s.9(2) in full English; the A1727 text only gives the substitution wording.

## 5. Penalties

### Takeaway
Failure to notify the Commissioner: fine up to RM250,000 or imprisonment up to 2 years, or both (s.12B(3)). Breach of any of the seven personal data protection principles (including Security Principle): amended s.5(2) now fine up to RM1,000,000 or imprisonment up to 3 years, or both, applying also to processors. Officer liability under s.133 is unchanged.

### Cited Findings
- s.12B(3): a data controller who contravenes s.12B(1) commits an offence; on conviction a fine not exceeding RM250,000 or imprisonment not exceeding 2 years or both. Note that the offence covers s.12B(1) only (notifying the Commissioner). There is no offence provision for s.12B(2) (notifying data subjects) in the statute. [PRIMARY] — A1727 s.6
- Circular 9 and Guideline 2.2 restate the RM250,000 / 2 years penalty for failure to notify the Commissioner under s.12B(1). [PRIMARY]
- s.5(2) before amendment: RM300,000 / 2 years / both. After A1727 s.4(b)(ii) (in force 1 Apr 2025): RM1,000,000 / 3 years / both; extended to a data processor who contravenes s.5(1A). Original s.5(1) obliges compliance with the principles in ss.6-12 (General; Notice and Choice; Disclosure; Security; Retention; Data Integrity; Access), so the amended penalty covers the Security Principle and the others equally; the Act does not set a separate Security tier. [PRIMARY] — A1727 s.4; [Act 709 reprint](https://www.pdp.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/UNDANG-UNDANG_AKTA_PERLINDUNGAN_DATA_PERIBADI_2010_709_MALAY_AND-ENG_V2022.pdf) (pre-amendment s.5(2); the reprint URL on the pdp.gov.my Act page is the V2022 file, name as listed on the site)
- Section 133 Act 709 (offences by body corporate) is not touched by A1727. If a body corporate commits an offence, any director, chief executive officer, chief operating officer, manager, secretary or similar officer (or person purporting to act, or responsible for management, or assisting in management) at the time may be charged severally or jointly, and is deemed to have committed the offence unless he proves it was committed without his knowledge, consent or connivance and that he took all reasonable precautions and exercised due diligence. [PRIMARY] — Act 709 s.133 (V2022 reprint); A1727 contains no amendment to s.133
- Secondary sources report the same RM250,000 / 2 year figure for s.12B and the RM1 million / 3 year figure for principle breaches. [SECONDARY] — e.g. [compliancehub.wiki](https://www.compliancehub.wiki/understanding-data-breach-notification-requirements-under-malaysias-pdpa)
- The Commissioner may investigate (s.105) and call for breach records (s.121) (Guideline 7.9-7.10). [PRIMARY]

### Inferences
- Failure to notify data subjects under s.12B(2) has no stated criminal penalty in A1727; exposure arises indirectly (investigation, Security Principle, enforcement notice). This is an inference from the text and should be confirmed with a lawyer.
- The statutory scale, restated: s.12B(3) RM250k/2y; s.5(2) RM1m/3y (all seven principles, controllers and processors); s.133 officer deemed liability with due diligence defence.

- No enforcement decisions on s.12B found; none reviewed.
