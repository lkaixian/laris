## 1. Notice and Choice Principle (s.7), consent, timing, language, chat-app first contact

### Takeaway
s.7 requires a written notice with eight content items, given "as soon as practicable" at first request/collection, in both Bahasa Malaysia and English, with a clear means to exercise choice. A1727 did not change s.7 or s.6. JPDP says the notice is not a consent vehicle: consent must be obtained separately, recorded and maintained. No JPDP rule specific to chat apps was found.

### Cited Findings
- s.7(1): data user must "by written notice" inform the data subject of: (a) that personal data is being processed by or on behalf of the user plus a description of the data; (b) purposes of collection and further processing; (c) any information available on the source of the data; (d) the right to request access and correction, and how to contact the user with inquiries or complaints; (e) the class of third parties to whom data is or may be disclosed; (f) the choices and means offered for limiting processing (including data of other persons identifiable from it); (g) whether supply of the data is obligatory or voluntary; (h) if obligatory, the consequences of not supplying it. **[P]** — Act 709 s.7 (PDF above)
- s.7(2) timing: notice "as soon as practicable" (a) when the data subject is first asked to provide data, or (b) when the user first collects the data; or (c) in any other case before using the data for a different purpose or before disclosing to a third party. **[P]** — Act 709 s.7(2)
- s.7(3) language: the notice "shall be in the national and English languages", and the individual must be given "a clear and readily accessible means to exercise his choice, where necessary, in the national and English languages". **[P]** — Act 709 s.7(3)
- A1727 touches neither s.6 nor s.7 (the Act's operative sections: s.2 data user to data controller rename; s.3 definitions incl. biometric data as sensitive; s.4 data processors bound by Security Principle; s.5 s.9 changes; s.6 DPO s.12A and breach notification s.12B; s.9 portability s.43A; s.12 s.129; s.13 electronic service). **[P]** — Act A1727 text
- Penalty for breaching the principles: fine up to RM300,000 and/or 2 years' imprisonment under s.5(2) in the pre-amendment Act, raised by A1727 s.4(b)(ii) to RM1,000,000 and/or 3 years; s.5(1a) makes data processors liable for the Security Principle. A1727 s.4 in force 1 April 2025. **[P]** — Act A1727 s.4; Gazette P.U.(B) 522
- s.6(1): non-sensitive personal data may not be processed without consent, subject to s.6(2) exceptions (contract performance, steps at the data subject's request toward a contract, legal obligation, vital interests, administration of justice, statutory functions). Sensitive personal data only per s.40 (explicit consent etc.). s.6(3): lawful purpose directly related to the user's activity, necessary, adequate but not excessive. **[P]** — Act 709 s.6. Note: A1727 added "biometric data" to "sensitive personal data" (A1727 s.3(c), in force 1 April 2025). **[P]**
- JPDP Notice Guide (Jan 2022): notice is mandatory for every data user regardless of type, form or size; failure carries the s.5 penalty. **[P]** — Panduan Penyediaan Notis PDP
- JPDP Notice Guide, "Reminder": the PDP Notice is only an information statement and "not a platform for the data user to obtain consent to process, especially blanket consent"; consent "must be obtained separately, recorded and maintained properly". **[P]** — same
- JPDP Notice Guide on when to deliver: when first collecting personal data; when using existing data for a purpose other than the original; when disclosing to third parties. **[P]**
- JPDP Notice Guide, recommended content beyond the statute: name the specific data items and say whether sensitive data is processed; say if data of children under 18 is processed; retention period and when data will be disposed of; practical security steps; source (internal/external); for third parties, name them and the reason for disclosure plus security measures; named contact officer with title, phone and email. **[P]** — same (these are guide recommendations, not statutory text)
- JPDP Notice Guide on language: must be bilingual (national language and English); notices in additional languages for business reasons are not an offence. **[P]**
- JPDP Notice Guide on delivery: plain, short style, headers, readable font; place where easily seen; for apps and websites, easily accessible and suited to the interface, not buried behind many links. Templates (Appendix A in BM, B in English) aimed at unregistered and micro/small/medium businesses. **[P]**
- CBPDT guideline 7.2-7.3: for consent-based transfers, the data controller must first give a notice stating the class of third parties receiving the data and the purpose of the transfer, then obtain the data subject's consent, which "shall be recorded and maintained properly in accordance with the Personal Data Protection Regulations". **[P]** — CBPDT paras 7.2, 7.3
- CBPDT guideline 4.3: a controller that transfers or intends to transfer data outside Malaysia must tell the data subject of the transfer through the PDP notice or other written notice. **[P]**
- Commercial-transaction forms: Retention Standard says to dispose of personal data collection forms used in commercial transactions within 14 days unless they carry legal value. **[P]** — Standard 2015, Retention Standard item 4

### Inferences
- A Telegram/WhatsApp bot must deliver the bilingual (BM + English) written s.7 notice, or a prominent link to it, at the first message that asks for or collects personal data (before storing anything beyond what is needed to deliver the notice), then a separate recorded opt-in. JPDP's "separate, not blanket" language suggests the chat consent prompt should not be bundled into the notice acceptance as a single tick.
- "Record" the consent: store at minimum user ID/handle, timestamp, notice version, language shown, and the exact consent text or button payload. The statute and guideline say "recorded and maintained" but I did not read the specific regulation on form (see Gaps).
- A person's chat messages may arrive before any notice; the safest design is a gated first-contact flow where the bot sends only the notice and consent request until consent is given.

### Gaps
- Regulation 3 of the Personal Data Protection Regulations 2013 (consent recording; I recall it requires consent in a form that can be recorded and kept, and for written consent that is bundled with other matters to be distinguishable, but this is memory only and NOT verified here): primary text could not be retrieved. Secondary sources not checked either. Lawyer should confirm.
- No JPDP guidance specific to messaging apps/chatbots/click-wrap or to implied consent found. The general PDPA Code of Practice (https://www.pdp.gov.my/akta/tataamalan-umum-perlindungan-data-peribadi/) was not opened.
- JPDP Public Consultation Paper 1/2026 on "AI and Personal Data Protection framework" is listed on the JPDP homepage dated 29 October 2026 (https://www.pdp.gov.my/kertas-konsultasi-awam-bil-1-2026-kerangka-kecerdasan-buatan-ai-dan-perlindungan-data-peribadi/). I did not read it; it may bear on the AI provider component and is only a consultation as of the homepage listing. **[P] existence only**
- Whether a draft Regulations amendment (Public Consultation Paper 4/2025, Aug-Sep 2025, https://www.pdp.gov.my/akta/kertas-konsultasi-awam-bil-4-2025-cadangan-pindaan-ke-atas-peraturan-peraturanperlindungan-data-peribadi-2013-p-u-a-335-2013/) has been finalised: not confirmed. One secondary web summary said it was still at consultation as of "August 2026" **[S]**, unverified.

## 2. Data subject rights: access, correction, withdrawal, prevent processing, portability; deadlines, fees, refusal

### Takeaway
Access (s.30-33) and correction (s.34-36) both have a 21-day deadline, extendable by at most a further 14 days with written notice. Withdrawal of consent (s.38) and a general right to prevent processing (s.42, s.43 direct marketing) exist by written notice. The new data portability right (s.43A, in force 1 June 2025) exists on paper, but the transmission period is "as may be prescribed" and I found no prescription or final guideline.

### Cited Findings
- Access: s.30(1) right to be told whether data is processed; s.30(2) requestor may "upon payment of a prescribed fee" make a data access request in writing for information on, and a copy of, the data. Separate request per separate entry held for different purposes (s.30(4)). **[P]** — Act 709 s.30
- Access deadline: comply "not later than twenty-one days from the date of receipt" (s.31(1)); if unable, before the 21 days expire send written notice with reasons and comply to the extent able (s.31(2)); comply in whole not later than 14 days after the 21-day period expires (s.31(3)). **[P]** — Act 709 s.31
- Access refusal grounds (s.32(1)): (a) insufficient information to verify identity (or relevant-person status); (b) insufficient information to locate the data; (c) burden or expense disproportionate to the privacy risk; (d) disclosure would reveal another identifiable individual's data (unless that person consents or it is reasonable to proceed); (e) another data user controls the processing so as to prohibit compliance; (f) would violate a court order; (g) would disclose confidential commercial information; (h) access regulated by another law. **[P]**
- Refusal must be notified in writing within 21 days with reasons (and the other data user's name/address if (e) applies) (s.33). **[P]**
- Correction: s.34 written correction request if data is inaccurate, incomplete, misleading or not up to date; s.35(1): if satisfied, correct, supply a copy of the corrected data to the requestor, and take practicable steps to send the correction (with reasons) to third parties who received the data in the preceding 12 months, all within 21 days; s.35(2) mirrors the access extension mechanism. **[P]** — Act 709 s.34-35 (s.35(2)-(5) and s.36 refusal grounds only partly read)
- Withdrawal of consent: s.38(1) data subject may "by notice in writing" withdraw consent; s.38(2) data user "shall, upon receiving the notice... cease the processing"; s.38(4) offence, fine up to RM100,000 and/or 1 year. Consequence of withdrawal: the Act text does not itself require deletion. **[P]** — Act 709 s.38
- Right to prevent processing likely to cause damage or distress: s.42 by written "data subject notice", requires cessation after a reasonable period if processing causes or is likely to cause substantial, unwarranted damage or distress; does not apply where the subject has consented (s.42(2)(a), partial text read). **[P]**
- Direct marketing: s.43 data subject may at any time by written notice require cessation of or non-commencement of direct marketing processing after a reasonable period; may complain to the Commissioner if not complied with. **[P]**
- Fees: the Personal Data Protection (Fees) Regulations 2013, P.U.(A) 338, in force 15 Nov 2013, set maximums of RM10 (access with copy), RM2 (without copy), RM30 (sensitive data with copy), RM5 (sensitive without copy). **[S]** — https://mia.org.my/box/2022/05/P.U.A.338.Personal.Data_.Protection.Fees_.Regulations.2013.pdf (I did not open the PDF; figures come from a web search summary of that document). Correction request fee not verified.
- Portability, text of new s.43A (inserted by A1727 s.9): (1) "a data subject may request the data controller to transmit his personal data to another data controller of his choice directly by giving a notice in writing by way of electronic means"; (2) the request is "subject to technical feasibility and compatibility of the data format"; (3) on receiving the request, the data controller "shall complete the transmission of personal data within the period as may be prescribed". **[P]** — Act A1727 s.9. A1727 s.3(e) also adds "data portability request" to the "requestor" definition. **[P]**
- Portability start date: Gazette P.U.(B) 522 (dated 19 Dec 2024, published 24 Dec 2024) brings A1727 sections 6 and 9 into force on 1 June 2025 (s.6 = DPO and breach notification; s.9 = s.43A portability). **[P]** — https://www.pdp.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/PENETAPAN-TARIKH-PERMULAAN-KUAT-KUASA.pdf
- Portability implementing material: JPDP's website lists only Public Consultation Paper No. 3/2024 on the Right to Data Portability (consultation, not a final guideline); no final portability guideline appears in the guidelines list on the A1727 page. **[P]** (absence on the pages I read) — https://www.pdp.gov.my/akta/kertas-konsultasi-awam-bil-03-2024-hak-kepada-kemudahalihan-data/ and https://www.pdp.gov.my/akta/akta-pdppindaan-2024/. I could not read the consultation PDF itself.
- Secondary agreement that portability is in force from 1 June 2025 and that the transmission period is not yet prescribed: Mayer Brown (July 2025) gives s.43A and the June 2025 start **[S]** — https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/07/from-legislative-reform-to-practical-guidance-key-amendments-to-malaysias-pdpa-and-the-launch-of-cross-border-transfer-guidelines ; DFDL **[S]** — https://www.dfdl.com/insights/legal-and-tax-updates/malaysia-implementation-of-the-personal-data-protection-amendment-act-2024/ . A web search summary said a commentary notes no period has been prescribed ("a right with no clock") **[S]**, unattributed and unverified.

### Inferences
- A SaaS should build an intake channel for written (including electronic, A1727 s.13 permits electronic service) access, correction, withdrawal and portability requests, with a 21-day clock logged, an identity check step (s.32(1)(a) ground), and a 14-day extension path.
- The statute says "in writing", so a chat command (e.g. /export or /forget) logged and confirmed is probably workable as the electronic written notice, but this is an inference, not verified by guidance.
- Because s.43A(3) defers to a "prescribed" period that I could not find, advise treating the access-style 21 days as a conservative internal target.

### Gaps
- Consolidated text of ss.35(2)-(5), 36, 37, 42(2) and 43 not fully read.
- Correction request fee, and whether the access-request fee applies to portability requests: not found.
- Whether any regulation or JPDP guideline has prescribed the s.43A(3) period as at 2026-10-30: not found; recommend lawyer checks pdp.gov.my "Peraturan" and any Gazette after June 2025.
- Contents of PCP 3/2024 (scope, conditions, proposed deadlines) not read.

## 3. Retention (s.10), data integrity (s.11) and erasure

### Takeaway
Malaysia has no standalone "right to erasure" for data subjects. It has a controller duty to not keep data longer than necessary and to destroy or permanently delete data no longer required (s.10), plus withdrawal of consent obliging cessation of processing (s.38) and the right to stop processing causing unwarranted damage (s.42).

### Cited Findings
- s.10(1): personal data "shall not be kept longer than is necessary for the fulfilment of that purpose"; s.10(2): duty of the data user "to take all reasonable steps to ensure that all personal data is destroyed or permanently deleted if it is no longer required for the purpose for which it was to be processed". **[P]** — Act 709 s.10. A1727 did not amend s.10 (s.5 amends s.9 only). **[P]**
- s.11 Data Integrity: reasonable steps so data is accurate, complete, not misleading and up to date, having regard to the purpose. **[P]** — Act 709 s.11
- s.12 Access Principle: data subject must be given access and be able to correct inaccurate data, except where refused under the Act. **[P]** — Act 709 s.12
- Retention Standard (Personal Data Protection Standard 2015, para 6): determine legal retention periods before destruction; keep no longer than necessary unless other laws require; keep a proper periodic record of data disposal, available to the Commissioner on request; dispose of commercial-transaction collection forms within 14 days unless of legal value; review and dispose of unwanted data in databases; prepare a disposal schedule for inactive data with a 24-month period; no removable media for storing personal data without written top-management approval. **[P]** — https://www.pdp.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/LatestStandard.pdf (English text; Standard predates A1727 and uses "data user")
- Data Integrity Standard (Standard 2015, para 7) exists; not read in detail. **[P]** existence only
- Withdrawal (s.38) requires cessation of processing; it does not on its face require deletion. **[P]**
- JPDP Notice Guide recommends stating retention period and disposal timing in the notice. **[P]**
- CBPDT para 16.3: cross-border transfer records must themselves be kept subject to the Retention Principle. **[P]**
- Secondary sources describing Malaysia as lacking an express GDPR-style erasure right were not retrieved; the conclusion above rests on the Act text I read (no erasure section among ss.30-43A). **[P]** by absence in the sections read; I did not read the whole Act line by line.

### Inferences
- A "/forget" feature is not a statutory requirement as such, but it is the practical way to satisfy s.38(2) (cease processing after withdrawal), s.10(2) (destroy when no longer needed after withdrawal removes the purpose/consent basis), and to evidence the disposal record the Standard asks for. Retention must be balanced against any legal retention duty (e.g. tax/accounting), which the Standard allows.
- Define in the notice a retention period per data category, and a deletion log.

### Gaps
- No JPDP numeric maximum retention period for general data beyond the 14-day and 24-month items above.
- Whether the amended Act or the draft Regulations (PCP 4/2025) add a deletion right: not found.

## 4. Cross-border transfer: amended s.129, CBPDT guideline, TIA, implications for the SaaS stack

### Takeaway
Since 1 April 2025 the Minister's gazetted whitelist (old s.129(1)) is gone. A controller may transfer data abroad if the destination has a substantially similar law or ensures an adequate level of protection at least equivalent to the PDPA (s.129(2)), or under a s.129(3) exception: consent, contract necessity, legal proceedings, adverse-action avoidance, "all reasonable precautions and due diligence" (typically via contractual clauses/BCR/certification), or vital interests. The public-interest exception (3)(h) is deleted. JPDP's CBPDT guideline (29 April 2025) says a Transfer Impact Assessment (TIA) is an optional but expected way to document the first two limbs, valid at most three years.

### Cited Findings
- A1727 s.12 amended s.129: deleted s.129(1) (the blanket prohibition except gazetted places); s.129(2) now reads "A data controller may transfer any personal data of a data subject to [a place outside Malaysia] if (a) there is in that place in force any law which is substantially similar to this Act; or (b) that place ensures an adequate level of protection in relation to the processing of personal data which is at least equivalent to the level of protection afforded by this Act" (A1727 s.12(b)(i)-(ii) deleted ", or that serves the same purposes as this Act" from (a)); s.129(3) exceptions retained but paragraph (h) (public interest, as determined by the Minister) deleted, with paragraph (g) now ending in a full stop and (f) ending "or"; s.129(4) (Commissioner's power over prohibitions on transfers of certain data) deleted; s.129(5) cross-reference changed from "subsection (1)" to "this section". The s.129 offence (fine up to RM300,000 and/or 2 years, pre-amendment s.129(5); penalty not changed by A1727) should be confirmed in the consolidation. **[P]** — Act A1727 s.12; Act 709 s.129 text
- Remaining s.129(3) exceptions: (a) data subject consent; (b) necessary for performance of a contract between data subject and controller; (c) necessary for conclusion/performance of a contract between controller and a third party at the subject's request or in the subject's interest; (d) legal proceedings/advice/rights; (e) reasonable belief that transfer avoids adverse action, consent is impracticable and the subject would have consented; (f) controller has taken all reasonable precautions and exercised all due diligence to ensure data will not be processed abroad in a way that would contravene the Act if processed in Malaysia; (g) vital interests. **[P]** — Act 709 s.129(3) as amended
- Effective date: A1727 section 12 (s.129) in force 1 April 2025. **[P]** — Gazette P.U.(B) 522
- CBPDT guidelines issued 29 April 2025 ("Tarikh Terbitan: 29 April 2025"); BM PDF on JPDP site. **[P]** — https://www.pdp.gov.my/akta/garis-panduan-pemindahan-data-peribadi-rentas-sempadan-cbpdt/
- CBPDT 5.3-5.7 (substantially similar law test): a law is substantially similar if its protections, rights and requirements on collection, disclosure, retention and cross-border transfer resemble those of Act 709; controllers "may" run a TIA to check; sources can include official publications, regulator decisions, intergovernmental/independent monitoring reports, research; TIA result valid "no more than three (3) years", then repeat; if the destination law changes during validity, review whether it remains substantially similar. **[P]**
- CBPDT 6 (adequate protection test, s.129(2)(b)): same TIA approach, 3-year validity and review on law change. **[P]**
- CBPDT 7: consent route requires prior notice (class of third parties and purpose), then consent recorded and maintained. **[P]**
- CBPDT 8-9: the "contract necessity" exceptions require a direct, objective link, for the core purpose of the contract; a transfer being merely convenient or an internal practice does not qualify. **[P]** (headings and paras 8.1-8.4 and 9.x read in part)
- CBPDT 12: the s.129(3)(f) "reasonable precautions and due diligence" route can be shown through (i) Binding Corporate Rules (intra-group), (ii) Contractual Clauses (CC), or (iii) certification under an approved scheme. Para 12.10 recommends reviewing the CC before using international model clauses (the guideline refers to international models; secondary sources list ASEAN Model Contractual Clauses and EU GDPR SCCs; I confirmed only the existence of 12.10 and the BCR content list, not the full CC list). **[P]** for the three mechanisms; **[S]** for named model clauses and certifications (Europrivacy, APEC CBPR/PRP) — Mayer Brown link above
- CBPDT 13: contains a rule on when the (f) route cannot be used/limits (13.1: transfers where the purpose is ... and a note on data-subject nationality at 13.1.x, partly read; not relied on). **[P]** existence only
- CBPDT 14-15: controller remains responsible for security in transit (Security Principle); contracts with third parties/processors must include data-security and processing clauses; the processor must comply with s.9. **[P]**
- CBPDT 16 (record keeping): keep a record of recipients: recipient name, company registration number (if any), contact details of recipient's DPO or other person; destination country; type of data transferred; purpose; other info as the controller sees fit. Also keep records proving each transfer complied with s.129: TIA records and findings (for s.129(2)); data protection notice and consent records (for (3)(a)); contract copies and proof of necessity (for contract exceptions); copies of BCR, recognised certificate, or signed contract between controller and recipient (for reasonable precautions). Records are themselves subject to the Retention Principle. **[P]**
- Mayer Brown: s.129 conditions, TIA factors (data subject rights, principles, DPO/breach requirements, processor obligations, regulator existence/powers; also recipient's legal or contractual obligations, security measures, certifications, compliance history), 3-year validity, record keeping. **[S]**, consistent with the primary guideline for the points I checked. Note this secondary source gives inconsistent paragraph numbering for the legal-proceedings exception (s.129(3)(d) vs "129(d)"); the Act text confirms 129(3)(d).
- The A1727 amendment to processors: data processors are directly bound by the Security Principle (s.5(1a), s.9 as amended), effective 1 April 2025. **[P]**

### Inferences (implications for Replit (US), RunPod, Mailtrap, ILMU)
- All four are, on the facts given, service providers/processors acting for the SaaS (the data controller). Replit (US) and RunPod and Mailtrap are likely foreign locations unless regions are chosen otherwise (RunPod and Mailtrap hosting locations were not researched); the AI provider ILMU being Malaysian-based means the data transfer to it is domestic, but sub-processors or hosting may still be foreign (not verified).
- For each foreign recipient, the lawyer needs a chosen s.129 basis. A US destination has no single federal law "substantially similar" to the PDPA, so a (2)(a)/(b) TIA for the whole US is a weak fit; the more practical bases are consent with prior notice naming the class of recipient and purpose (3)(a) plus a recorded consent, and/or (3)(f) via signed contractual clauses or certification (e.g. a DPA incorporating model clauses). Whether a particular provider holds a recognised certification (APEC CBPR/PRP) was not researched.
- Contract necessity (3)(b) is narrow per the guideline and unlikely to cover hosting, email delivery or AI inference purely for convenience.
- Maintain a transfer register per CBPDT 16 (recipient, registration no., DPO contact, country, data types, purpose, evidence) and name recipients or their class in the notice (s.7(1)(e) and CBPDT 4.3).
- A DPIA/TIA record kept at least 2 years (see Q5).

### Gaps
- Official English version of CBPDT not read; full list of recognised model clauses and certification schemes (CBPDT para 12.5-12.13 detail) not read line by line.
- Consolidated post-A1727 text of s.129(4)-(6) (including offence penalty) not verified.
- No JPDP decision, case, or enforcement example on US cloud transfers found.
- Provider-specific facts (Replit, RunPod, Mailtrap, ILMU hosting regions and their DPAs, certifications) were outside this research scope and were not checked.

## 5. Other JPDP instruments relevant to a small SaaS, and effective dates

### Takeaway
Besides notice and cross-border, the amendment introduced mandatory DPO appointment and breach notification (both from 1 June 2025), and JPDP has issued a DPIA guideline (2026) with numeric triggers a small SaaS may not meet. Whether these apply depends on facts (volume, sensitivity).

### Cited Findings
- Phased commencement of A1727 (Gazette P.U.(B) 522, dated 19 Dec 2024, published 24 Dec 2024): 1 Jan 2025 = A1727 ss.7, 11, 13, 14 (administrative: s.16(3) wording, s.67 funds, electronic service s.136(1)(aa), saving clause); 1 April 2025 = A1727 ss.2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 10, 12 (rename to data controller, new definitions incl. biometric data and personal data breach, processors under the Security Principle, higher penalty, s.21 amendment, deletion of s.48(e), amended s.129); 1 June 2025 = A1727 ss.6 and 9 (DPO s.12A, breach notification s.12B, portability s.43A). **[P]** — Gazette and A1727 text.
- s.12A (in force 1 June 2025): each controller (and processor) must appoint one or more DPOs accountable for compliance and notify the Commissioner. **[P]** — A1727 s.6. ### Inferences
- A small SaaS below 20,000 data subjects would not trip the quantitative DPIA threshold but should still consider the qualitative factors and the cloud-storage illustration; doing a lightweight DPIA is explicitly called best practice.
- The DPO and breach obligations apply from 1 June 2025 to controllers regardless of size per the statutory text (s.12A has no size carve-out in the Act); the Circular may set thresholds for DPO exemptions, not verified.
